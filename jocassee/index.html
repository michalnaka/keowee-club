Lake Jocassee, the deep one

Keowee's wild upstream sibling: a mountain reservoir ringed by protected gorges, three times deeper, with waterfalls you can drive a boat to. Here's the water, the depth, and how to get on it.

Why the level swings

Jocassee is the upper half of Duke's pumped-storage battery: water runs downhill to Keowee to make power when demand spikes, and gets pumped back up when it's cheap. A lake that's 15 feet down in one season and brimming the next is normal operations, not drought.

Live gauges & history

What "down" means here

Keowee holds within a few feet of full; Jocassee routinely works a 0 to 23 foot range. Lower water uncovers red-clay banks and stretches the walk down the ramp — but the waterfalls keep falling and the water stays that impossible blue-green.

By the numbers

SCDNR + Duke Energy figures

157 ft average · ~351 ft max

South Carolina's deepest lake. The bottom of the old Whitewater River channel sits some 350 feet under your hull — deep enough that divers visit a drowned lodge, and deep enough to stay cold at the bottom all summer.

7,500 acres · 75 mi of shoreline

Less than half Keowee's size, and almost none of the shoreline is developed — the lake sits inside the Jocassee Gorges, tens of thousands of acres of protected mountain land.

Born 1973 · full pond 1,110 ft

Duke Energy impounded the Whitewater, Toxaway, Horsepasture, and Thompson rivers to build its pumped-storage system. Under the water: the old Attakulla Lodge and the valley the lake is named for.

Waterfalls you can boat to

the reason to come

Rivers pour off the Blue Ridge Escarpment straight into the lake, which means waterfalls with boat parking. All of them are on our 3D map — fly the lake tour passes every one.

Laurel Fork Falls

By boatNE arm

The postcard one — about 80 feet, dropping right into a cove at the lake's northeast end. Idle in close on calm mornings; kayakers camp nearby on the Foothills Trail.

What's under the water

the drowned valley

Before 1973 this was the Jocassee Valley — a resort valley with a lodge, a girls' camp, a church, and family farms. Duke flooded it with most of the buildings still standing, and the cold, dark water has kept them. Divers have been finding them ever since.

Attakulla Lodge

~300 ft downSince 1904

The valley's resort hotel, built around 1898, took in traveling families for half a century. It went under intact in 1973 and was found by diver Bill Routh in 2004 — still standing, roof about 270 feet below the surface. Only technical divers ever see it: 46-degree water, total darkness, 15 minutes on site, two hours of decompression coming back up.

Mount Carmel Baptist Church cemetery

~130 ft downDeliverance, 1972

The graveyard-relocation scene in Deliverance was filmed here while the lake was actually filling. Families were given the choice to move their dead; many chose to leave them be. Divers report headstones still upright, some names still legible.

Camp Jocassee for Girls

~300 ft down1922–1960s

Generations of Upstate girls spent their summers in the valley from 1922 until the late 1960s. The stone entrance pillars and the white picket fence are still down there on the old valley floor.

Getting on the water

one way in

Devils Fork State Park

Salem, SCOnly public ramp

The only public boat access on Jocassee — ramps, villas, camping, and a swim area. Summer weekends fill by mid-morning, so launch early. When the lake is well below full, call the park office before towing to check ramp conditions.

Open in MapsPark info

No boat? Still worth it

Kayak and pontoon rentals operate from the park in season, and guided waterfall tours run most of the year. The water is famously clear — trout-cold at depth, swimmable up top all summer.

Quick answers

How deep is Lake Jocassee?

157 feet on average, about 351 feet at maximum per SCDNR — South Carolina's deepest lake. For comparison, neighboring Keowee averages 53 feet.

What's the water level today?

The live chip at the top of this page reads Duke's gauge right now, in feet below the 1,110-foot full pond. History and both lakes' gauges live on the lake level page.

Why is Jocassee so low sometimes?

Pumped storage: Duke runs water downhill to Keowee for power and pumps it back overnight. Multi-foot swings are routine and reverse themselves — it's an energy schedule, not a drought alarm.

Can you swim in Lake Jocassee?

Yes — the water is exceptionally clear and comfortable at the surface in summer, with a designated swim area at Devils Fork. It gets cold fast below the surface layers; this is a trout lake at depth.

Where do you launch?

Devils Fork State Park in Salem is the only public access. Arrive early on summer weekends.
